What Is Islamic Finance?

Islamic finance is a financial system based on principles derived from Shariah. One of its best-known principles is the prohibition of Riba (interest).

Instead of relying solely on conventional interest-based lending, Islamic finance can use structures such as Murabaha, Mudarabah, Ijara and Qard Hasan, depending on the nature of the transaction and applicable Shariah requirements.

Islamic finance also emphasizes ethical investment, transparency, risk sharing, real economic activity and avoidance of prohibited business activities.

At Alkhair Islamic Finance, these principles form the foundation of our approach to ethical financing.

Our Shariah-Oriented Principles

1. No Riba

Islamic finance prohibits Riba, commonly understood as interest. Our stated objective is to provide financing structures based on Sharia-compliant principles rather than conventional interest.

2. Murabaha

Murabaha is a cost-plus financing structure in which the transaction involves the purchase and resale of an asset with the relevant profit disclosed according to the applicable agreement.

3. Mudarabah

Mudarabah is a partnership-based model involving capital and entrepreneurial effort, with profit sharing according to an agreed arrangement and applicable Shariah principles.

4. Ijara

Ijara is a leasing-based Islamic finance structure in which an asset is leased to the customer according to agreed terms.

5. Qard Hasan

Qard Hasan refers to a benevolent, interest-free loan concept used for qualifying purposes under Islamic finance principles.

The exact structure applicable to any financing product should always be confirmed from the final agreement and applicable Shariah documentation.
